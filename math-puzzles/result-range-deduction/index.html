Math Puzzles: Deduction by Result Range

Classic results span 10 to 99. Bracketing the result with three greens turns the board into arithmetic bookkeeping — the fastest narrowing method in the game.

In this topic · The bracketing sequence · From bounds to templates

The bracketing sequence

Three board facts bound the result before any candidate is checked: the equals position (which fixes the result's digit budget), the leading digit (which bounds it from one side), and the operator set (which bounds it from the other). The result bounds guide formalizes the sequence; this page shows the real numbers the bounds live in.

Results below 10 or above 99 are impossible by construction. When a candidate implies a result outside the range — or outside the digit budget the equals position dictates — it is eliminated without computation. This is the cheapest filter in the game: it costs one glance per candidate.

From bounds to templates

A bracketed result plus a known operator pair leaves a handful of operand templates. Enumerating those templates — not digits — is the move: each template either matches the remaining greens exactly or dies. The candidate enumeration guide walks the procedure, and the worksheets build the arithmetic speed it depends on.

The payoff is endgame behavior: boards solved by bracketing rarely need more than one probe, because the bracket itself has already separated the candidates into groups that distinct probes can split. Guessing boards and bracketing boards feel like different games — the difference is whether the first three deductions ran.

Worked example from the bank

Take the equation 78-41=37. Reading it under the site's rules: high-precision operators resolve first, the left side must equal 37 exactly, and every division step stays integer. Reconstruct the deduction: an equals green at slot 6, a leading-digit green on 7, and the operator set implied by the pair table — the candidates that survive are few, and the board finishes by bookkeeping rather than guessing. This is the standard shape of a solved Numble board: three structural reads, then arithmetic.
