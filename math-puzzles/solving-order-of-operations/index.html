Math Puzzles: Solving by Order of Operations

How standard precedence shapes every valid equation in the 9833-equation classic bank — with the measured operator-pair table showing which structures the puzzle actually generates.

The precedence contract

Every equation in the bank evaluates under standard precedence: multiplication and division bind before addition and subtraction, equal-precision operations run left to right. This is not a convention here — the generator only accepts candidates whose left side, evaluated under these rules, equals the right side. A candidate that "looks true" left to right but breaks under precedence is mathematically false and cannot be the answer.

The consequence for solvers: before any candidate is submitted, evaluate the high-precision operations first. Across the 9833 classic equations, 24% contain a multiplication and 4% contain a division — every one of those requires the two-phase read. +× dominates with 652 equations (7%), followed by ×+ (652) and ++ (645). Rare pairs — the ones with counts under 200 — are endgame probes, not early commits: when the pool is wide, prefer candidates from the common pairs, and only test rare-pair structures when the greens demand it.

Precedence as elimination

The same contract eliminates candidates for free. Any candidate mixing × or ÷ with + or − can be checked in two seconds: compute the high-precision cluster, then the rest. A mismatch kills the candidate outright — no further arithmetic needed. Stack this check before comparing candidates by feel, and roughly a third of plausible-looking rows die without costing a guess.

Mini mode runs the identical contract on six slots (206 candidates). The compressed geometry makes precedence errors more visible — fewer slots to hide a mis-order — which is why the mini pool is the fastest place to train the two-phase read.

Reading order at the board

Practical sequence: (1) scan the candidate for × and ÷, compute those clusters first; (2) resolve + and − left to right; (3) compare with the right side. Ten boards of this drill install the reflex — the worksheets on this site train exactly this computation order, and the precedence pitfalls guide catalogs the four failure patterns that survive it.

Worked example from the bank

Take the equation 78-41=37. Reading it under the site's rules: high-precision operators resolve first, the left side must equal 37 exactly, and every division step stays integer. Reconstruct the deduction: an equals green at slot 6, a leading-digit green on 7, and the operator set implied by the pair table — the candidates that survive are few, and the board finishes by bookkeeping rather than guessing. This is the standard shape of a solved Numble board: three structural reads, then arithmetic.
