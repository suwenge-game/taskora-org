Practice sequencing for this material

Skills from this page land in this order: first run the drill on a worksheet set (timed, untidy is fine), then apply it to the daily equation the same day, then teach it back — a written sentence in your notes explaining the rule to a hypothetical beginner. The teaching-back step is the retention mechanism; skips there decay within a week.

Expect a three-day consolidation window per skill. Day one feels mechanical, day two feels ambiguous, day three it starts firing unprompted. Players who quit on day two never reach the payoff, which is why this site spaces its worksheet sets rather than batching them — spacing is the method, not a compromise.

Fair-difficulty guarantee

The daily equation is generated under constraints this site documents openly: integer division results, standard precedence, and a deterministic daily seed. No guesswork spikes, no hidden difficulty days. If a board feels unfair, check two things before suspecting the puzzle: every division in your candidate must be integer-clean at each step, and every mixed-operator reading must follow standard precedence. Those two checks resolve the overwhelming majority of "impossible" boards.

The same constraints make the site's advice durable: because the generator's rules are fixed and published, techniques derived from them cannot be patched out from under you. What changes is the calendar, not the mathematics.

Using the library with the daily puzzle

The library's eight guides map onto the eight phases of a typical solve: reading the board structure (equals placement, two-operator grammar), verifying candidates fast (multiplication shortcuts, division integrity), avoiding false reads (precedence pitfalls), and narrowing the endgame (result bounds, candidate enumeration). Before a daily, pick the one guide whose skill matches the phase you most often lose; after the daily, note whether that guide's technique fired. One guide at a time beats the full library every session — attention is the scarce resource in a six-row solve.

The guides deliberately share a vocabulary — anchors, brackets, templates, enumeration — so that practicing one reinforces the others. If a term feels undefined on your first read, the gloss is in an earlier guide; the suggested reading order in the list above minimizes backtracking. Readers who prefer jumping in mid-library will find each guide self-contained enough, with the shared vocabulary re-glossed on first use.

Editorial standard for the numbers

Every structural claim in the library (operator-pair frequencies, equals-position rules, division rarity) is computed from the same generator the daily uses, and the generator's rules are published on the research page. Claims about browser arithmetic are checked against the site's own mini and classic modes. Corrections follow the editorial policy's log; a correction on a formula page is dated, not silently patched — the audit trail matters as much as the accuracy.
