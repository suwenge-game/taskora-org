How to use this set

Timing protocol

Set a three-minute timer for Part A and ninety seconds for Part B. The target is completion with accuracy, not speed alone; a rushed sheet with two wrong answers is a failed session. Log the date and time taken — the log turns ten worksheets into a visible skill curve.

Why these four styles

The daily equation draws from the same arithmetic families as this sheet: single products for the multiplication-heavy templates, precedence pairs for the mixed-operator answers, clean division for the integer-constraint boards, and same-precision chains for the left-to-right traps. Fluency here is fluency on the board — nothing on the sheet is arithmetic for its own sake.

After the timer

For any missed item, redo it by digit-sum verification instead of re-computation. The verification habit catches future board errors in two seconds, which is the entire point of Part B. A miss you fix by verification trains the reflex; a miss you fix by re-computing trains nothing.

How this connects to the daily equation

Everything on this page feeds the daily puzzle in one of two ways: either it sharpens a verification skill (the arithmetic you run before submitting a row) or it sharpens a narrowing skill (the deduction that cuts the candidate pool). Before your next daily, decide which of the two you are weaker at — verification errors show up as wrong equations that felt right; narrowing errors show up as rows that teach nothing.

The equation bank generates candidates under strict rules: integer results, standard precedence, an equals sign no earlier than the middle slot. Every page here exploits one of those rules as a constraint — and the generator's consistency is why the constraint-based approach beats guessing. When a rule-based elimination says a candidate is impossible, it is impossible; the board will not surprise you.

A practical study loop: read one section, play the daily, then write one sentence about where the section's idea applied (or honestly note that it did not). Ten days of one-sentence notes is a personalized strategy document — better than any generic guide because it is annotated with your own board history. The worksheets zone exists to drill the verification half of the loop; the hint archive on our sister sites models what a worked deduction chain looks like.

Difficulty expectations

This site keeps a single difficulty curve: the daily is always solvable by the methods documented here, in roughly the documented number of rows. There are no trick days and no RNG spikes — the generator's constraints (integer division, precedence-clean structures) exist precisely to keep the difficulty fair. If a board feels unfair, the likeliest cause is a missed constraint: re-check division integrality and precedence order before blaming the puzzle.

Mini mode compresses the same mathematics into six slots, which makes it the fastest place to practice any page's skill: fewer slots mean each deduction fires sooner. Players preparing for a difficult classic week often warm up on mini boards specifically because the feedback loop is tighter.
