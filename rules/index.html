How to play

Find the hidden, mathematically true equation in no more than six submitted rows.

In this guide · Play · Daily · Strategies · Scoring

1. Understand the structure

Classic mode uses exactly eight characters; Mini uses six. Every digit, operator, and the equals sign occupies one position. The left side must contain an operation, while the right side is its non-negative whole-number result. For example, 12+34=46 fills eight positions and is true. A row with the wrong length cannot be submitted.

2. Build a legal equation

Allowed characters are digits 0–9, +, −, × (*), ÷ (/), and exactly one equals sign. Both sides must be present and equal. Multi-digit numbers cannot start with zero, so 03 is not a legal way to write 3. The game also rejects division by zero, negative final answers, decimals, and division with a remainder.

Valid: 12+34=46. Invalid: 12+34=45 because the two sides do not match.

3. Apply order of operations

Multiplication and division are evaluated before addition and subtraction. That means 2+3×4 equals 14, not 20. Operations at the same level are read from left to right. Exact division matters at the step where it occurs: an expression is rejected if a division step produces a fraction, even if later arithmetic could appear to hide it.

4. Read the feedback

Green:
the character is correct and already in its exact position.
  • Gold: the character appears in the answer but belongs in another position.
  • Gray: no unused copy of that character remains in the answer.

    • Repeated characters are counted precisely. If an answer contains one 4 and your guess contains two, at most one copy receives a matching color. Treat each green as fixed, move gold characters to open positions, and avoid gray characters unless duplicate feedback proves another copy is still possible.

    5. Know the end conditions

    You win by placing every character correctly within six valid guesses. Invalid input is explained before it consumes a row. A Daily win contributes to the calendar streak; a loss ends that attempt. Practice play is useful for testing ideas without waiting for tomorrow.

    Practice sequencing for this material

    Skills from this page land in this order: first run the drill on a worksheet set (timed, untidy is fine), then apply it to the daily equation the same day, then teach it back — a written sentence in your notes explaining the rule to a hypothetical beginner. The teaching-back step is the retention mechanism; skips there decay within a week.

    Expect a three-day consolidation window per skill. Day one feels mechanical, day two feels ambiguous, day three it starts firing unprompted. Players who quit on day two never reach the payoff, which is why this site spaces its worksheet sets rather than batching them — spacing is the method, not a compromise.

    Fair-difficulty guarantee

    The daily equation is generated under constraints this site documents openly: integer division results, standard precedence, and a deterministic daily seed. No guesswork spikes, no hidden difficulty days. If a board feels unfair, check two things before suspecting the puzzle: every division in your candidate must be integer-clean at each step, and every mixed-operator reading must follow standard precedence. Those two checks resolve the overwhelming majority of "impossible" boards.

    The same constraints make the site's advice durable: because the generator's rules are fixed and published, techniques derived from them cannot be patched out from under you. What changes is the calendar, not the mathematics.
